## MODULE 1: Sentence Basics — *Repair the Broken Message*
**Core Topic:** Subject and Predicate  
**Target Duration:** 6–7 minutes

### 1. Verified Source Citations
- **Odisha BSE — *LEARN AND PRACTISE GRAMMAR CLASS - IX***: Ch. 1 *Parts of a Sentence*, Sec. A–B, Book pp. 1–7 (PDF pp. 7–13) — word order, Subject/Predicate, head-word, split interrogatives, understood *you* in imperatives, omitted *it/is* in exclamatives.
- **Assam SEBA — *A NEW METHOD English Grammar For Class IX & X***: Ch. 1 *Sentence*, Lessons 1–6, Book pp. 1–9 (PDF pp. 10–18) — word order, Subject (*उद्देश्य*), Predicate (*विधेय*), Finite verb governed by Subject's Number/Person, Subject Adjuncts, Objects & Complements.
- **Punjab PSEB — *GRAMMAR IN USE Class IX***: Ch. 5 *Simple and Complex Sentences*, Book p. 53 (PDF p. 59) — one-line mention (*"one subject and one predicate"*).
- **Odisha BSE — *Learn and Practise Grammar Class - X***: Ch. 2 *Types of Sentences*, Book pp. 13–15 (PDF pp. 19–21) — assumed Subject/Predicate foundation expanded into Finite Verb & `SV / SVO / SVOO / SVC / SVOC` patterns.

### 2. Knowledge Provenance Breakdown
- **Textbook-Taught Knowledge:** Meaningful word order; Subject as who/what the sentence is about; Predicate as what is said about the Subject (always containing the finite verb); head-word identification; understood *(You)* in imperatives; split predicates in questions (`Do the students read... ?`).
- **Inferred Prerequisites (Not Re-Taught in Full):** Basic recognition of naming words (Nouns/Pronouns) and action/being words (Verbs) from middle school.
- **Original / Supplementary Content:** School noticeboard repair story with Mira, Arjun, and Gyan; Visual Teaching Engine word-tile separation into Subject Zone and Predicate Zone; Class 10 bridge extending multi-word predicates into basic clause patterns (`SV`, `SVO`, `SVC`); all healthy-food/heritage sentences and 10-MCQ bank.

### 3. Learning & Assessment Objectives
- **Grammar Outcome:** Separate complete sentences into Subject and Predicate, identify the core head-noun in an enlarged Subject, and recognise non-standard Subjects (imperatives and inverted questions).
- **Knowledge Outcome:** Discover interesting facts about Indian agriculture, nutrition, and regional life through repaired noticeboard messages.
- **Assessment Objectives (10 MCQs):** Distinguish complete sentences from fragments; identify full Subject vs Subject head-word; identify full Predicate; locate understood *(You)* in imperatives; identify split Subject/Predicate in questions; distinguish Class 10 complement vs object extensions where applicable.

### 4. Step-by-Step Learning Sequence

#### Step 1 — Prerequisite Check (45 sec)
- Quick 2-tap warm-up: Students unscramble 4 word tiles (`water` / `drink` / `Children` / `clean`) into `"Children drink clean water."` (citing Odisha IX Ch. 1 Sec. A Act. 1 word-order pattern) and tap the naming word (`Children`) and action word (`drink`).

#### Step 2 — Situation & Challenge (45 sec)
- **Scene:** At school, wind tears Gyan's noticeboard announcements in half, leaving fragments like *"Fresh mangoes from the orchard..."* and *"...carry heavy books to the library."*
- **Challenge (Before Rule Explanation):** Mira asks the student to pick which missing half completes *"Fresh mangoes from the orchard"* into a clear sentence (`taste sweet and healthy` vs `in yellow baskets`).

#### Step 3 — Simple Explanation + Textbook-Checked Rule (60 sec)
- **Plain Language:** Every complete sentence has two jobs: (1) name **who or what** we are talking about, and (2) tell **what they do or are**.
- **Textbook-Checked Rule (Odisha IX Ch. 1 p. 5; Assam IX–X Ch. 1 pp. 2–3):**
  - **Subject:** The part that names the person, place, animal, or thing spoken about. Its core word is a **head-noun** (or pronoun).
  - **Predicate:** The part that says something about the Subject. It **always contains the finite verb**.
- **Class Differentiation:**
  - **Class 9 Focus:** Single-word and enlarged noun-phrase Subjects (`The tall farmer in the field`), split predicates in questions (`Does Arjun drink milk daily?`), and understood `(You)` in imperatives (`Wash your hands before meals.`).
  - **Class 10 Focus:** Rapid review of head-words + identifying **Verb + Object / Complement** inside the Predicate (`SVO` vs `SVC`, grounded in Odisha X Ch. 2 pp. 14–15) so students are ready for Module 3 SVA.

#### Step 4 — Worked Examples (Visual Grammar Teaching Engine) (60 sec)
- **Worked Example 1 (Correct Analysis):**
  - Sentence: *"The young gardener waters the mango saplings every morning."*
  - Engine highlights `[Determiner: The] [Adjective: young] [Noun/Head-word: gardener]` → **Subject** ("Who is this about?").
  - Engine highlights `[Verb: waters] [Noun Phrase: the mango saplings] [Adverbial: every morning]` → **Predicate** ("What does the gardener do?").
- **Worked Example 2 (Common Mistake):**
  - Mistake: Selecting only `"gardener"` as the *entire* Subject, or treating `"of the village"` in `"The farmers of the village grow rice"` as part of the Predicate.
  - Correction: Modifiers stay attached to the Subject (`The farmers of the village`), while `"farmers"` is the **head-word** that will control the verb later.

#### Step 5 — Story Checkpoints / Guided Practice (90 sec)
1. **Checkpoint 1 (Enlarged Subject):** Repair *"The crisp apples in the basket / keep us healthy."* — Tap where the Subject ends and Predicate begins. Immediate feedback explains why *"in the basket"* describes the apples (Subject modifier).
2. **Checkpoint 2 (Imperative & Question):**
   - Class 9: *"Read one science chapter every evening."* → Choose the hidden Subject (`You (understood)`).
   - Class 10: *"Have the students in Class 10 visited the planetarium?"* → Identify the Subject trapped inside the split predicate (`the students in Class 10`).

#### Step 6 — Independent Practice (60 sec)
- 3 quick interactive items (tile-split, head-word tap, fragment vs sentence check) using sentences such as *"Rani Lakshmibai defended Jhansi with great courage."* and *"Fresh curd and rice give steady energy."* Immediate concise feedback per item.

#### Step 7 — 10-MCQ Assessment Blueprint (Post-Submit Review Only)
- **Items 1–2:** Identify a complete sentence vs a Subject-only or Predicate-only fragment *(Odisha IX Ch. 1 pp. 1–4)*.
- **Items 3–4:** Select the **complete Subject** in a sentence with pre/post modifiers *(Odisha IX Ch. 1 pp. 5–6; Assam Ch. 1 pp. 4–6)*.
- **Items 5–6:** Identify the **head-word of the Subject** inside a prepositional noun phrase *(Odisha IX Ch. 1 Act. 4–7)*.
- **Items 7–8:** Identify the **complete Predicate** (including split interrogative predicates) *(Odisha IX Ch. 1 pp. 6–7)*.
- **Item 9:** Identify the understood Subject `(You)` in an imperative instruction *(Odisha IX Ch. 1 p. 7; Assam Ch. 1 p. 3)*.
- **Item 10:** Class 9: Identify the finite verb inside the Predicate *(Assam Ch. 1 p. 3)*. / Class 10: Distinguish `Verb + Object` vs `Verb + Complement` inside the Predicate *(Odisha X Ch. 2 pp. 14–15)*.
- **Reward on Completion:** Learning Star Badge & Virtual Book/Fruit Basket Milestone.

## MODULE 2: Tenses — *Journey Through Time*
**Core Topic:** Time and Verb Forms  
**Target Duration:** 7–8 minutes

### 1. Verified Source Citations
- **Odisha BSE — *LEARN AND PRACTISE GRAMMAR CLASS - IX***: Ch. 3 *Time and Tense*, Book pp. 33–61 (PDF pp. 39–67) — **2-Tense model** (Present & Past; *"no future tense in English"*), `v / v-ed / -ed/-en` forms, 8 Tense × Aspect forms, stative verbs (p. 43), Future Time expressions (pp. 57–61).
- **Odisha BSE — *Learn and Practise Grammar Class - X***: Ch. 1 *Tense Revisited*, Book pp. 1–12 (PDF pp. 7–18) — reaffirms 2 tenses + 3 time phases; 6 ways to express Past Time; 5 ways to express Future Time.
- **Punjab PSEB — *GRAMMAR IN USE Class IX***: Ch. 9 *Tenses (Concord and Sequence)* Sec. 1 & 3, Book pp. 94–97, 100–104 (PDF pp. 100–103, 106–110) — **12-Tense model** (Present/Past/Future × Indefinite/Continuous/Perfect/Perfect Continuous) + Sequence of Tenses.
- **Assam SEBA — *A NEW METHOD English Grammar For Class IX & X***: Ch. 9 *Tense*, Book pp. 120–147 (PDF pp. 129–156) — Time vs Tense (L1), 3 Tenses / 12 sub-tenses (L2–3), Strong/Weak/Mixed verbs (L4), conjugation tables (L5), non-continuous verbs & 15 Common Errors (L6).

### 2. Knowledge Provenance Breakdown
- **Textbook-Taught Knowledge:** Time (past, present, future) is a real-world timeline, whereas Tense/Verb Form is how verbs show time and aspect; regular vs irregular past/participle forms; Present Simple for habits/facts vs Present Progressive/Continuous for ongoing action; Past Simple vs Present Perfect (`for/since`) vs Past Perfect (earlier past event); expressing future time (`will`, `be going to`, Present Progressive/Simple); non-progressive/stative verbs (`know, understand, belong`).
- **Inferred Prerequisites:** Recognising the finite verb inside the Predicate (from Module 1) and Subject number (`I/he/they`).
- **Original / Supplementary Content:** Transparent **Board-Model Bridge Note** (explaining why Odisha calls future forms *Future Time Expressions* under a 2-Tense model while PSEB/Assam call them *Future Tense* inside a 12-Tense chart, without erasing either model); ISRO/diary timeline interactive slider; Class 10 Sequence-of-Tenses / Past-Perfect timeline contrast; healthy-food/science sentences and 10-MCQ bank.

### 3. Learning & Assessment Objectives
- **Grammar Outcome:** Distinguish real-world **Time** from grammatical **Verb Form**, select accurate Present, Past, and Future-time verb forms for a given context, and avoid stative-verb progressive errors.
- **Knowledge Outcome:** Trace milestones of Indian space science (ISRO's Chandrayaan and Mangalyaan missions) and daily healthy routines across a timeline.
- **Assessment Objectives (10 MCQs):** Match time markers (`yesterday`, `every morning`, `since 2022`, `tomorrow`) to accurate verb forms; distinguish Present Simple vs Present Progressive; distinguish Past Simple vs Present Perfect / Past Perfect; select natural Future Time expressions (`will`, `going to`, timetabled Present Simple); reject progressive forms of stative verbs.

### 4. Step-by-Step Learning Sequence

#### Step 1 — Prerequisite Check (45 sec)
- Student inspects *"ISRO engineers test the satellite signal."* (from Module 1) and taps the finite verb (`test`), then sees how changing the time clue (`yesterday`) forces the verb (`tested`) to change.

#### Step 2 — Situation & Challenge (45 sec)
- **Scene:** Arjun drops his science-club journal pages. Three entries about India's space programme and his daily morning routine lack matching verb forms on the timeline (`Past` — `Present` — `Future`).
- **Challenge (Before Rule Explanation):** Student drags a timeline marker to `"Yesterday morning"` and picks which sentence fits: *"Vikram reads the book"* vs *"Vikram read the book"* vs *"Vikram will read the book"*.

#### Step 3 — Simple Explanation + Textbook-Checked Rule (75 sec)
- **Plain Language:** **Time** is when an event happens (Past, Now, Future). **Verb form (Tense & Aspect)** changes the verb or adds helpers (`is`, `has`, `had`, `will`) to show *when* and *whether the action is finished or ongoing*.
- **Textbook-Checked Distinction (Transparent Multi-Board Note):**
  - **Time vs Tense:** All boards agree **Time** has 3 phases (Past, Present, Future), and that Present Simple can describe a scheduled future event (*"The train leaves at 6 a.m. tomorrow"*).
  - **Two Accepted School Classifications:**
    - *Odisha BSE Model (IX Ch. 3 pp. 33–34; X Ch. 1 p. 1):* Verbs inflect for **2 Tenses (Present & Past)**; future time is expressed using modals (`will/shall`), `be going to`, Present Progressive, or Present Simple.
    - *PSEB IX & Assam SEBA Model (PSEB Ch. 9 pp. 94–97; Assam Ch. 9 pp. 121–126):* Organises usage into **3 Tenses × 4 Aspects = 12 Tenses** (*Present/Past/Future* × *Simple/Indefinite, Continuous/Progressive, Perfect, Perfect Continuous/Progressive*).
  - Grammar Quest teaches the **shared verb-form patterns and contexts** clearly while showing both label conventions (`Progressive / Continuous`, `Future Time / Future Tense`).
- **Class Differentiation:**
  - **Class 9 Focus:** Present Simple vs Present Progressive, Past Simple vs Present Perfect (`for/since`), Stative verbs, Future Time (`will` vs `be going to` vs timetabled Present Simple).
  - **Class 10 Focus:** Deepening *Tense Revisited* (Odisha X Ch. 1 pp. 1–12) + *Sequence of Tenses* (PSEB IX Ch. 9 pp. 100–102): contrasting **6 ways to express Past Time** (especially **Past Perfect** for the earlier of two past events) and reported-clause backshift exceptions (universal truths stay Present Simple).

#### Step 4 — Worked Examples (Visual Grammar Teaching Engine) (60 sec)
- **Worked Example 1 (Correct Analysis — Present Simple for Habit vs Present Progressive for Now):**
  - *"Mira drinks a glass of warm milk every morning."* (`drinks` = `[Verb: Present Simple]` → daily habit).
  - *"Look! Mira is drinking fresh water after her run."* (`is drinking` = `[Aux Verb: is] + [Main Verb-ing: drinking]` → happening right now).
- **Worked Example 2 (Common Mistake — Stative Verb & `for/since`):**
  - Incorrect: *"Arjun is knowing the answer since morning."* (Citing Odisha IX Ch. 3 p. 43 & Assam Ch. 9 pp. 142–144 Common Errors).
  - Correction: *"Arjun has known the answer since morning."* (`know` is a mental/stative verb, not normally used in `-ing` progressive form; `since morning` links past to present → Present Perfect).

#### Step 5 — Story Checkpoints / Guided Practice (90 sec)
1. **Checkpoint 1 (Timeline Shift):** *"India's Mangalyaan spacecraft entered Mars orbit in 2014."* → Move marker to **Past** and choose the accurate form (`entered` vs `has entered` with finished past time `in 2014`).
2. **Checkpoint 2 (Future Time Schedule vs Plan):** *"The school science exhibition ___ at 9 a.m. tomorrow."* → Select `opens` (timetabled future) and read why Present Simple expresses fixed future schedules (Odisha IX Ch. 3 p. 59; Assam Ch. 9 p. 139).
3. **Checkpoint 3 (Class-Tiered Past Contrast):**
   - Class 9: Choose `have lived` vs `lived` with `for five years` (ongoing to now).
   - Class 10: *"When we reached the observatory, the eclipse ___ already ___."* → `had ... begun` (earlier past event).

#### Step 6 — Independent Practice (60 sec)
- 3 rapid-response items:
  1. Habitual scientific truth (*"Green plants ___ sunlight to make food."* → `use`).
  2. Ongoing vs completed past action (*"While Subhas Chandra Bose ___ at Cambridge, he studied civil service papers."* / everyday school narrative).
  3. Rejecting stative progressive (*"This brass telescope ___ to our school laboratory."* → `belongs`, not `is belonging`).

#### Step 7 — 10-MCQ Assessment Blueprint (Post-Submit Review Only)
- **Item 1:** Universal scientific truth → Present Simple *(Odisha IX p. 37; PSEB p. 94)*.
- **Item 2:** Action happening at the moment of speaking → Present Progressive/Continuous *(Odisha IX p. 41; Assam p. 138)*.
- **Item 3:** Stative verb trap (`understand / own / seem`) rejecting `-ing` *(Odisha IX p. 43; Assam p. 142)*.
- **Item 4:** Completed past action with explicit past marker (`last week / in 1928`) → Past Simple *(Odisha IX p. 49; X p. 4)*.
- **Item 5:** Action continuing from a past starting point up to now with `since` / `for` → Present Perfect / Present Perfect Progressive *(Odisha IX pp. 44–48; Assam p. 143)*.
- **Item 6:** Interrupted past action (`was reading ... when the bell rang`) → Past Progressive + Past Simple *(Odisha X p. 5; PSEB p. 95)*.
- **Item 7:** Class 9: Intention/prediction with evidence (*"Look at those dark clouds; it ___ soon."* → `is going to rain`). / Class 10: Earlier of two past actions → Past Perfect *(Odisha X pp. 6–7; PSEB p. 96)*.
- **Item 8:** Timetabled future event (*"The Rajdhani Express ___ at 7:30 a.m. tomorrow."* → `departs`) *(Odisha IX p. 59; X p. 9; Assam p. 139)*.
- **Item 9:** Class 9: `used to` for discontinued past habit *(Odisha IX p. 50)*. / Class 10: Sequence of Tenses universal-truth exception (*"The teacher reminded us that the earth ___ round the sun."* → `moves`) *(PSEB p. 101)*.
- **Item 10:** Identifying why a sentence's verb form mismatches its time clue (`yesterday` + `will go`).
- **Reward on Completion:** Time Explorer Badge & Virtual Library Shelf Book.

## MODULE 3: Subject–Verb Agreement — *The Grammar Detective*
**Core Topic:** Subject–Verb Agreement (Concord)  
**Target Duration:** 7–8 minutes

### 1. Verified Source Citations
- **Odisha BSE — *Learn and Practise Grammar Class - X***: Ch. 3 *Subject – Verb Agreement*, Book pp. 23–30 (PDF pp. 29–36) — Core Number/Person rule + **16 numbered rules** + Activities 1–3; Ch. 4 *Non-Finite Verb Forms*, p. 31 (finite verb agrees with subject).
- **Punjab PSEB — *GRAMMAR IN USE Class IX***: Ch. 9 Sec. 2 *Concord: Agreement of the Verb with Subject*, Book pp. 97–100 & Ex. 2–6 pp. 102–104 (PDF pp. 103–106, 108–110) — **13 numbered rules + exceptions**.
- **Odisha BSE — *LEARN AND PRACTISE GRAMMAR CLASS - IX***: Ch. 2 p. 32 (PDF p. 38) basic person/number table; Ch. 7 pp. 83–89 (PDF pp. 89–95) countable/uncountable, pair nouns (`trousers` vs `a pair of`), `police/people/cattle`, `-s` uncountables (`Mathematics/Diabetes`); Ch. 8 pp. 93, 98–99 (PDF pp. 99, 104–105) quantifiers & `The rich/blind`.
- **Assam SEBA — *A NEW METHOD English Grammar For Class IX & X***: Ch. 1 p. 3 (finite verb agreement), Ch. 4 Ex. 4 pp. 50–51 (`news/physics` vs `cattle/people`), Ch. 5 pp. 66–67 & 73 (`and`, `each/every`, `either...or/neither...nor`, `each of/neither of`), Ch. 6 pp. 80 & 84 (`The Secretary and Headmaster` vs `two determiners`; `Many a man`), Ch. 12 p. 201 (`Bread and butter`).

### 2. Knowledge Provenance Breakdown
- **Textbook-Taught Knowledge:** Verb must agree with the **Subject (head-noun)** in **Number** (singular/plural) and **Person** (1st/2nd/3rd); intervening phrases do not change the Subject's number; `and` (two distinct items = plural; single idea/one person with one determiner = singular); `each/every/either/neither/one of` + singular verb; `or/either...or/neither...nor/not only...but also` agree with the **nearer subject**; `with/as well as/together with` follow the **first subject**; `-s`-ending singular subjects (`Mathematics, Physics, news, measles`); plural-only nouns (`police, cattle, people, scissors`) vs `a pair of...`; single-unit measurements/sums; `a number of` (plural) vs `the number of` (singular); relative pronoun antecedent (`who/which/that`).
- **Inferred Prerequisites:** Locating the **Subject** and its **head-word** even when followed by a prepositional phrase (Module 1), and knowing singular/plural verb forms such as `is/are`, `was/were`, `has/have`, `v+s/v` (Module 2).
- **Original / Supplementary Content:** School festival announcement detective storyline with Mira, Arjun, and Gyan; lock-and-magnet visual pairing between Subject head-noun card and Verb card; explicit **Class 9 vs Class 10 staged scope** (resolving the gap where Odisha Class IX only previews SVA while PSEB IX teaches 13 rules); transparent notes on Odisha X's informal plural note for `neither of/none of` vs standard exam singular; healthy-food/heritage sentences and 10-MCQ bank.

### 3. Learning & Assessment Objectives
- **Grammar Outcome:** Detect and fix Subject–Verb mismatches by tracing past intervening words, compound joiners, collective/measurement nouns, and tricky `-s` subjects back to the true grammatical Subject.
- **Knowledge Outcome:** Edit school event posters about regional Indian crafts, balanced nutrition, and national freedom-struggle figures (`Bhagat Singh`, `Chandrashekhar Azad`, `Rani Lakshmibai`).
- **Assessment Objectives (10 MCQs):** Apply core singular/plural concord; ignore prepositional/intervening traps; handle `and` (compound vs single idea/one determiner); apply `either...or / neither...nor` (nearest subject) and `as well as / with` (first subject); classify `-s` singular nouns, `police/cattle`, `a pair of`, `each of / one of`, unit sums, and Class 10 `a number of / the number of` & relative antecedents.

### 4. Step-by-Step Learning Sequence

#### Step 1 — Prerequisite Check (45 sec)
- Student taps the **head-word** inside *"The basket of ripe bananas ___ on the table."* → highlights `basket` (not `bananas`), activating Module 1's head-word skill.

#### Step 2 — Situation & Challenge (45 sec)
- **Scene:** Posters for the School Heritage & Health Fair contain detective clues—several verbs clash with their Subjects (`"A basket of fresh mangoes are ready"`).
- **Challenge (Before Rule Explanation):** Gyan points at `"The quality of these apples (is / are) excellent."` Student chooses before seeing the rule.

#### Step 3 — Simple Explanation + Textbook-Checked Rule (75 sec)
- **Plain Language:** A verb does **not** agree with the nearest noun by accident—it locks onto the **true Subject (the head-word)**.
- **Textbook-Checked Rules (Odisha X Ch. 3 pp. 23–29; PSEB IX Ch. 9 pp. 97–100; Odisha IX Ch. 2, 7, 8; Assam Ch. 4–6):**
  1. **Core Rule:** Singular subject → singular verb (`is, was, has, eats`); Plural subject → plural verb (`are, were, have, eat`).
  2. **Intervening Words Trap:** Phrases like `of the students`, `with her friends`, or `as well as his teammates` do **not** make a singular Subject plural.
  3. **Compound Joiners:**
     - `A and B` (two people/things) → **plural**; *Single idea* (`Slow and steady`, `Dal and rice as one dish`) or *one determiner for one person* (`The captain and wicketkeeper`) → **singular**.
     - `Either...or / Neither...nor / Not only...but also` → verb matches the **nearer subject**.
  4. **Tricky Nouns & Indefinites:**
     - `Mathematics, Physics, news, measles` → **singular**; `ten kilometres / five hundred rupees` (one unit) → **singular**.
     - `police, cattle, people` & `scissors/trousers` → **plural**, whereas `a pair of scissors` → **singular**.
     - `each / every / each of / one of / either of / neither of` → **singular** *(Transparent note: Odisha X Ch. 3 p. 26 notes informal plural usage with `neither of/none of` in speech, whereas formal school grammar across PSEB, Assam, and Odisha exam items uses singular).*
- **Class Differentiation:**
  - **Class 9 Tier:** Core Person/Number, Intervening `of`-phrases, `and` compound vs single idea, `with/as well as`, `either...or / neither...nor`, `-s` singular nouns (`news, Mathematics`), `police/cattle`, `a pair of`, `each/every/one of`.
  - **Class 10 Tier:** All Class 9 rules **plus** `a number of` (plural) vs `the number of` (singular), fractions/percentages (`Two-thirds of the land is...` vs `Two-thirds of the apples are...`), `the + adjective` (`The brave`), collective-noun unit vs individuals (`The jury was unanimous` vs `The jury were divided`), plural book/country names (`Gulliver's Travels`), and relative pronoun antecedents (`one of the students who work hard`).

#### Step 4 — Worked Examples (Visual Grammar Teaching Engine) (60 sec)
- **Worked Example 1 (Correct Analysis — Intervening Phrase):**
  - Sentence: *"A crate of fresh oranges arrives at the canteen every Tuesday."*
  - Engine dims `[Preposition + Noun Phrase: of fresh oranges]`, draws a lock-line from `[Head-Noun: crate (Singular)]` to `[Verb: arrives (+s)]`.
- **Worked Example 2 (Common Mistake — `as well as` vs `neither...nor`):**
  - Mistake: *"Arjun as well as his classmates are visiting the museum."*
  - Correction: *"Arjun as well as his classmates **is** visiting the museum."* (Unlike `and`, `as well as` leaves the Subject as `Arjun` — singular). Contrast with *"Neither Arjun nor his classmates **were** late."* (Verb locks onto the nearer plural noun `classmates`).

#### Step 5 — Story Checkpoints / Guided Practice (90 sec)
1. **Checkpoint 1 (Head-Noun Lock):** *"The stories of Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad (inspires / inspire) young readers."* → Lock onto `stories` (plural) → `inspire`.
2. **Checkpoint 2 (Tricky Look-Alikes):**
   - *"A pair of reading glasses (is / are) lying on Mira's desk."* → `is` (`pair` is singular).
   - *"Physics (help / helps) us understand light and motion."* → `helps`.
3. **Checkpoint 3 (Class-Tiered Connector):**
   - Class 9: *"Each student in the choir (carry / carries) a songbook."* → `carries`.
   - Class 10: *"The number of solar panels on our school roof (has / have) doubled."* → `has`.

#### Step 6 — Independent Practice (60 sec)
- 3 Detective Cards where mismatched verbs shake apart and matched ones snap together:
  1. *"Rice and dal (is / are) a wholesome daily meal across India."* → `is` (single dish/meal concept).
  2. *"Either the captain or the players (need / needs) to sign the register."* → `need` (nearer plural subject).
  3. *"The cattle (is / are) grazing beside the river bank."* → `are` (plural noun `cattle`).

#### Step 7 — 10-MCQ Assessment Blueprint (Post-Submit Review Only)
- **Item 1:** Intervening prepositional phrase (*"The colour of these ripe mangoes ___ bright yellow."* → `is`) *(PSEB p. 97; Odisha X p. 25)*.
- **Item 2:** `and` compound vs one-person single article (*"The poet and scholar ___ arrived at the hall."* → `has`) *(Odisha X p. 26; Assam p. 80; PSEB p. 98)*.
- **Item 3:** Single-unit idea/food (*"Slow and steady ___ the race."* / *"Curd and rice ___ soothing in summer."* → `wins` / `is`) *(Odisha X p. 24; PSEB p. 97)*.
